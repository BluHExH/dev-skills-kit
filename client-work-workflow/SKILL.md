# Client Work Workflow

## Requirement Gathering

Always clarify before estimating or starting:
- What is the core problem the client is trying to solve?
- Who are the end users?
- What does success look like? (measurable outcomes)
- What is the deadline and budget range?
- Are there existing designs, brand guidelines, or technical constraints?
- Who will provide content, assets, and feedback?

Ask open questions and summarize back to the client for confirmation.

## Project Estimation

Break work into clear phases or features.
Provide ranges when uncertainty is high (e.g. 8-12 days).
Include buffer for revisions and unexpected issues (usually 15-25%).
Separate design, development, testing, and deployment time.

## Quotation / Proposal Structure

1. Understanding of the problem
2. Proposed solution overview
3. Scope of work (what is included)
4. Out of scope items
5. Timeline and milestones
6. Pricing and payment terms
7. Assumptions and dependencies
8. Next steps

Keep language clear, professional, and free of jargon unless the client is technical.

## Client Communication

- Prefer written confirmation for important decisions.
- Send regular progress updates (weekly is usually good).
- When delayed, communicate early with a new realistic timeline.
- Never promise features or deadlines you cannot meet.
- Document feedback and change requests.

## Progress Reporting

Simple weekly update format:
- What was completed this period
- What is planned next
- Blockers or risks
- Questions needing client input

## Delivery Best Practices

- Deliver working software frequently, not one big release at the end.
- Provide clear handover documentation.
- Offer a short warranty or support period after launch.
- Ask for testimonials or case study permission after successful delivery.
